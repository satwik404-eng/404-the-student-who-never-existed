# Game design

The player is a witness investigating a university archive that erased researcher Aarya Sen. ECHO begins as an ambiguous digital assistant and is eventually revealed as Aarya's recovery routine. ARCHIVIST erased the researcher who found unauthorized edits.

## The Purge and the disputed operator

The first full cinematic follows authenticated name registration. Public preview visitors see only a transmission teaser. The anonymous operator is permanently obscured. Its apparent deletion is presented as an **ARCHIVIST audit reconstruction**, with an explicitly unverified overlay clock. The player is therefore shown an accusation that can be tested, rather than an authoritative omniscient account.

The mirror packet, independent storage times, denied purge permission and ordered audit receipts progressively undermine the caption. Chapter5 identifies 0xNULL as Aarya’s emergency preservation identity and ECHO as its recovery routine. ARCHIVIST substituted its own deletion caption and timestamp. The postgame recovered perspective explains these observable contradictions against the same opening scene.

The suspect board distinguishes known facts from contradictions. Dr.Voss provides an alternate access lead and recovery ticket; access alone never establishes guilt. Provisional theories and evidence connections are browser-local notes with no XP or progression effect.

## Investigation loop

Read the objective, inspect a real interactive workspace, submit a discovery, receive server validation, collect evidence, and open the next chapter. Incorrect answers are retryable. Every prerequisite is enforced by the backend as well as the interface.

The free field guide states the current task without giving a solution. Optional hints are server-only until purchased. Previously purchased hints remain accessible after refresh. They explain methods rather than provide answers.

## Chapters

| Chapter | Mechanic | Available evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The first trace | Inspect the original HTML comment, or use the safe mobile source mirror | Deliberate recovery marker in `index.html` |
| The ghost signal | Decode the supplied Base64 string with an interactive UTF-8 decoder | Encoded signal shown as selectable text |
| The impossible timeline | Find activity after deletion using same-clock UTC timestamps | Readable records and raw JSON served after chapter two |
| The hidden operator | Read three numbered fragments in a fictional filesystem | `help`, `ls`, `pwd`, `cd`, `cat`, `grep`, `history`, `clear`, `whoami` |
| The false confession | Put four UTC audit slips in chronological order | Timestamps printed on each card; drag or keyboard-accessible arrow buttons |
| The final protocol | Reconcile the recovered student ID within a three-minute connection window | Original identity card in the evidence journal |

The final timeout never deletes completed evidence. Restarting an expired connection grants a new attempt window. Once the identity is verified, the player may choose restoration or disclosure. Both endings can be explored without duplicate chapter rewards.

## Scores

Each completed chapter grants 100 XP. A correct first submission grants another 25 XP. Hint purchases cost 10, 20 and 30 XP cumulatively. Negative interim scores are permitted so that hints always have their documented cost. No arbitrary score is accepted from the browser.

Ranks: NOVICE below 250; ANALYST from 250; INVESTIGATOR from 450; GHOST HUNTER from 600; ARCHIVE MASTER from 700. The maximum clean score is 750 XP. Completion time measures wall-clock time from starting the investigation to the first ending.

## The Split File

The host receives a sequence of labels. Their partner receives the corresponding words. Neither response contains both halves. Both must be present and ready to start. Chat is literal text; no other player is simulated. Cooperative completion is separate from the single-player leaderboard.

Rooms hold two players, retain at most 60 messages, expire after 24 hours, and are limited to three retained rooms and three creations per account per hour. Quotas are shared across browser slots. Leaving during an active mission closes and reclaims the room.

## Presentation and compatibility

Six distinct introductions use source sheets, a signal waveform, drifting records, an obscured operator, conflicting evidence connections and a warning-red AI core. Introductions are marked seen per account/browser/investigation only after Continue or Skip. Completed legacy chapters open directly; their intros can be replayed explicitly. Recovery events replay without calling the submission endpoint.

The final server countdown begins after the chapter6 introduction is dismissed. Cinematic skip/intercept calls the original idempotent start operation; replay does not call it. Both endings retain the original completion time and derived rankings. All optional effects have text, reduced-motion and muted alternatives.
